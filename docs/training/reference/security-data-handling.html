Security and data handling

TaxDesk holds national tax identifiers, financial records, and client documents. The software does a lot to protect them. These are the habits it relies on people to keep.

For everyone

  1. Never share your account. Every action is recorded under your name.
  2. Keep MFA on a phone only you use. Store recovery codes in the firm's vault or a locked drawer, never in email or chat.
  3. Sign out on shared computers, and lock your screen when you step away.
  4. Treat exports as sensitive. Report CSVs and downloaded documents contain client data. Keep them on firm devices and delete them when done.
  5. Do not copy identifiers out of TaxDesk into notes, chat, or spreadsheets. TaxDesk never shows full CNIC/NTN for a reason.
  6. Only upload files from known sources. Documents are not virus-scanned.
  7. Report anything odd straight away: codes you did not request, someone else's name in your activity, or records you should not see.

For administrators

  • Deliver activation and reset links privately, one to one. They are valid for 30 minutes and work once. Never post them in group channels or tickets.
  • Give the smallest role that works. Review users monthly. Deactivate leavers on their last day.
  • Keep exactly two or three administrators, each with MFA.
  • Watch audit history for auth.login_failed spikes and unexpected exports.
  • Use a firm-owned Microsoft account for OneDrive, protected with MFA. Never browse or rearrange the TaxDesk app folder by hand.

For operators

  • Separate secrets per environment, stored in Cloudflare and GitHub and in an offline vault. Never in git, chat, or tickets.
  • Never run auth:recover or migrations against a non-test database without approval, and record why.
  • Prefer interactive prompts: command-line passwords end up in shell history.
  • Delete decrypted backup files (restore.dump) as soon as a restore is verified.
  • Use a least-privilege database role for the app, and a read-only one for backups.

Test and training data

Synthetic only, everywhere except production

Local, staging, demos, screenshots, bug reports, slides, and this guide use invented data only. Use ops:sample-import or the example templates: CNICs in the impossible 00000 region, emails at example.test. Never "borrow" a real client to make a demo look realistic.

What TaxDesk does for you

Sign-inPBKDF2 password hashes, mandatory TOTP, rate limiting, a Turnstile challenge after repeated failures, generic error messages
SessionsSecure, HTTP-only, SameSite=Strict __Host- cookie; 30-minute idle and 12-hour limits; revoked on role or security changes
AuthorizationChecked on the server for every request; a hidden record and a missing record look the same
IdentifiersCNIC/NTN encrypted, with a separate keyed index for exact search; always masked on screen and in audit
StorageOneDrive tokens encrypted; app-folder-only access; every document open re-checked and audited
IntegrityAppend-only audit and status history; immutable finalized invoices and payments; version checks on edits
BrowserContent Security Policy and security headers; Origin checks on changes
LogsStructured logs carry only request IDs and error classes, never secrets, tokens, or document contents
